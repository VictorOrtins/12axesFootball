About 45% of partners don't rank first. Team→manager is the weakest link type (held-out MRR 0.58), plausibly because a manager's vector covers a whole career while a team covers one era.

**Suggested decision:** keep the current constants (candidate A). Then re-enable `ScorerBenchmarkTest` with floors recalibrated on football, using the values above. Note that the center check is ~19–21% on football: with 15 "centered" profiles out of only 25 ideologies it can hardly pass, so its `CENTERED_PROFILES` should scale with the catalog size.

The match formula in `ProfileMatchScorer` has hand-picked numbers that were calibrated on the political catalogs of the original project. Nobody has checked them against the football catalogs. `ScorerBenchmarkTest` has been `@Disabled("aguarda os catalogos de futebol")` since the fork, and the catalogs now exist (25 ideologies, 65 managers, 79 teams).

The goal is to find numbers we can justify for football, or to show that the current ones are already fine, and to turn the benchmark back on as a guard.

## Next options, most to least likely to work

Tuning the 8 constants gave nothing, so the remaining gains have to come from the data or from a different formula. "Works" means a gain on the held-out pairs that is outside the seed noise (about ±0.01 MRR) without lowering the guardrails. Each option should be prototyped in Python and measured with `tune_scorer.py`'s pairs and guardrails before any Java change.

1. **One vector per manager spell.** Team→manager is the weakest link type (held-out MRR 0.58). A manager has one vector for a whole career, while a team is one era. Score a manager by their best-matching spell. It targets the biggest measured gap and doesn't touch the formula. Cost: a new audit per spell. The spells are already listed in `spellsCounted` in each manager's research file.
2. **Use each profile's uncertainty.** The research files record `gaps` and `confidence`. Axes a profile's research couldn't support should count less in its matches; today a guessed axis counts as much as a documented one. It's cheap to try, but how well it works depends on how consistently `gaps` were filled in.
3. **Likelihood matching.** Treat the user's vector as a noisy measurement of a profile and score how likely the answers are under it. The noise comes from the quiz itself (3 or 5 Likert questions per axis), so short-quiz users get wider uncertainty than extended-quiz users. It replaces the hand-built formula with one derived from the quiz, and it combines with option 2. Bigger change: the guardrails and the shown percentage would need rechecking from scratch.
4. **Learn the distance from the pairs (metric learning).** Fit per-axis weights, or a matrix that also captures correlations between axes, with a learning-to-rank loss. With only 127 pairs it is likely to overfit, the same problem the Optuna search faced with 8 numbers, now with 12 or more. Worth it only if the gain holds on the held-out pairs.
5. **Calibrate the percentage shown.** Map the raw score to something people can read, such as how the match compares with typical users. It doesn't change the ranking, so it can't improve the pair results. It only makes "87% compatible" mean something, and it can be done alongside any of the above.

- **No variant met every guardrail floor at any w;** each lowered at least one guardrail a little. The guardrails moved by less than about 1 point, so this says the floors are strict more than that the terms do damage.

**Audit noise is large.** The control audit differs from the catalog vector by about 10 points per axis (so about 7 points for a single audit), and a re-audit of the same manager typically doubles or halves the rank (Coutinho 14 → 30, Scolari 6 → 22, Pedro Emanuel 20 → 6). That is as large as a quiz user's own noise (6.5–8 points with 5 questions, 9–12 with 3). It probably also explains why tuning the formula found a flat landscape: the vectors set the ceiling. Averaging 2–3 audits per profile would cut that noise by a quarter to a half, and no formula change does that.

**Caveats, from most to least important.**

1. **Part of the gain is built in.** The spell audit and the team audit describe the same club in the same years, written by the same kind of model from the same general knowledge. Agreement between them is partly shared knowledge, not a better picture of the manager. The pairs measure consistency, not what users get.
2. **The test chose the spell for each manager.** In the product, every spell competes for every query, and a manager with 7 spells gets 7 chances to match. The extra chances can lift managers who have nothing to do with the query. This is not measured here.
3. **Only 15 pairs, picked for ranking badly.** 
4. **Three spell audits (Pedro Emanuel, Cardoso, Coutinho) had no tactical evidence in the research file** and answered from general knowledge. Two of them did not improve.

**Verdict.** Promising but unproven: the spell vectors clearly beat today's vectors, but they only beat a plain second audit by a margin that could be noise, and the cost side (caveat 2) was untested at that point. Not worth the full 184 audits yet without two cheap checks, which were then run (results below):

- Simulate caveat 2 without new audits: give each manager one noisy copy of its vector per counted spell (noise matching the 7 points measured above), score by the best copy, and check the other 57 pairs and the guardrails. This gives the price of taking the best of several draws.
- Test audit averaging: re-audit the same 14 managers once more, average the two audits, and see whether the pairs rank better than with one audit.

## Why not just tune for noise recovery

If we add random noise to each profile and tune until it finds its own source, the best answer is "nearest profile by distance". The tuning would drop direction and magnitude because of the noise we assumed, not because of anything about football. So noise recovery is a guardrail here, not the main goal.

## The football signal: researched pairs

The research files link profiles to each other, and those links were researched separately from the vectors:

- Ideology → exemplar managers and teams (`exemplars` in `profile-audit/research/football-ideology/*.json`). 55 of 87 links point to a profile that is in the catalog.
- Team-era → its managers (`managers[].managerId` in `profile-audit/research/team/*.json`). 75 of 94 links are in the catalog.

That gives about 130 pairs. A good formula ranks the true partner high. For example, gegenpressing's vector scored against all managers should put Klopp and Rangnick near the top.

The main goal is the mean reciprocal rank (MRR) of the true partner across these pairs, plus the share of pairs where the partner is in the top 3.

## What Optuna optimises

Optuna only searches. We write one function that takes a candidate set of the 8 constants and returns the numbers below.

### Objective 1: true partners rank high (maximise)

For each pair (query → partner, for example gegenpressing → Klopp):

1. Score the query's vector against every profile in the partner's catalog (all 65 managers) with the candidate constants.
2. Sort by compatibility and find the partner's rank.
3. The pair scores 1/rank: 1st = 1.0, 2nd = 0.5, 3rd = 0.33, 10th = 0.1.

The objective is the average over the tuning pairs (MRR). Report the top-3 hit rate next to it, because it's easier to read.

- **Run simulated users as the query, not only the clean vector.** Use several noisy quiz takes of the query profile (step 4) and average over them. This measures what the app does: a noisy user against clean profiles.
- **Weight each link type equally.** Average ideology→manager, ideology→team and team→manager separately, then combine, so the type with the most pairs doesn't dominate.

### Objective 2: the formula still behaves sensibly (constraints, or a second objective)

These are the `ScorerBenchmarkTest` checks. Each catches a failure that objective 1 can't see:

| Check | Failure it catches |
|---|---|
| Recovery under noise | A profile retaking the quiz with noise doesn't find itself |
| Opposite rejection | A user's mirror image scores high |
| Center rejection | Extreme users get matched to centrist profiles |
| Extremity inflation | Lukewarm users get perfect scores with extreme profiles (the bug the magnitude term fixed) |
| Stability | A tiny change in answers flips the top match |
| Discrimination | Every score crowds into 85–90%, so first and second look the same |

MRR only looks at the order of the matches, so it would accept constants that keep the right order but squash every score into a narrow band. Users see those percentages, so the checks have to cover the spread too.

### Combining them

- **Default:** the checks are hard limits, with the values the current constants reach on the football catalogs as the floor. Optuna maximises MRR among the candidates that pass.
- **To see the trade-off:** add a guardrail score as a second objective. NSGA-II then returns the Pareto front, for example "+0.08 MRR costs 3 points of noise recovery".

Both objectives are based on ranks, so they move in steps and have no useful gradients. TPE and NSGA-II don't need gradients.

## Steps

1. **Environment.** Create a venv at `~/.venvs/optuna` with `optuna` and `numpy`. It is a dev tool only: the backend never depends on it.
2. **Python copy of the scorer.** Start from the deleted `profile-audit/compatibility.py` (see History): swap in the football axis ids and drop the religion filter. Keep it as a module that `tune_scorer.py` imports. Check it against Java scores for a fixed set of vector pairs, to 0.1. Java can't be recompiled on this machine (no `javac`), so take the reference numbers from a test run in CI or from a machine with JDK 21.
3. **Pairs.** Load the pairs above from the research files and drop links to profiles that aren't in the catalog. Split them into about 70% for tuning and 30% held out, with a fixed seed, so a profile's pairs don't land on both sides.
4. **Simulated quiz users.** For each profile, take its audited answers (`profile-audit/answers/`), flip some answers by one or two Likert steps, and rebuild the vector with `profile_vector.compute_vector`. Use both the `short` (3 questions per axis) and `extended` (5) variants. This copies the steps of 0.25 a real user produces.
5. **Guardrails.** Port the `ScorerBenchmarkTest` metrics to the script: recovery under noise, opposite rejection, center rejection, stability, discrimination between first and second, and extremity inflation control for lukewarm users.
6. **Optuna study.** Use the objectives from "What Optuna optimises": MRR on the tuning split, with the guardrails as constraints (or as a second NSGA-II objective). Sample 4 raw weights and divide each by their sum, so they always add up to 1. Keep each other constant within a sensible range around today's value. Repeat over a few simulation seeds.
7. **Pick a point.** Show the Pareto front (the trade-offs where no goal can improve without another getting worse), Optuna's parameter importances, and three candidates. Compare them with the current constants and with "distance only" on the held-out pairs.
8. **Decide (user):** which candidate to ship, or to keep the current numbers.
9. **Ship.**
   - Update the constants in `ProfileMatchScorer`.
   - Update `ProfileMatchScorerTest` with values derived from the constants.
   - Recalibrate the limits in `ScorerBenchmarkTest` on the football catalogs and remove `@Disabled`.
   - Update the formula and the benchmark numbers in `MATCH_EXPLAINED.md`.

## Open decisions (user)

- **Per-dimension matches** (tactics, philosophy, management) use the same formula on 2 to 6 axes. Should they share the tuned constants (simpler, suggested) or get their own study?
- **How much churn is acceptable.** New weights change many results. The report should say how many top-1 results change for the simulated users and for the researched profiles.

## Risks

- **About 150 profiles is little data.** Thousands of trials will find noise that happens to fit. The held-out pairs and several seeds are there to catch this.
- **The pairs share a source with the vectors.** The same auditor answered the quiz for every profile, so its habits can link profiles that shouldn't match. The pairs are a better signal than noise recovery, but they aren't ground truth.
- **An exemplar isn't the same as a perfect match.** A manager can be an exemplar of an ideology and still differ from it on management axes. Ranking the partner in the top few is a better goal than ranking it first.

## Done when

- The study ran, and the report compares the candidates, the current constants and distance only on the held-out pairs.
- The user chose a candidate (or to keep the current numbers).
- The chosen constants are shipped and the benchmark is enabled and passing.
- `MATCH_EXPLAINED.md` is updated.
